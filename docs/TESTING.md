# Testing and evaluation

LocalAILine is tested at two levels: fast automated tests of the code, and **spoken test calls** that run the whole system (speech recognition, the AI, the voice, the business apps) the way a real caller would.

## Automated tests

```bash
cd app
flutter analyze
flutter test $(ls test/*_test.dart test/scenarios/*_test.dart | grep -v live_test)
```

These cover:

- **Business apps** (`apps_test`, `website_mcp_test`, `app_pro_test`, `app_pro2_test`): for every template, website forms save and refuse what they should; there's no double-booking; closed days, stock, capacity and delivery fees work; callers' tools find only their own bookings; the manager endpoints need the PIN.
- **Security** (`security_test`): attacks on a running app server (see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)).
- **Calls** (`call_end_test`, `said_days_test`): when a call may end; how names, numbers, days and teammates' names (even misheard) are understood; what's never said aloud.
- **Screens** (`live_calls_view_test`, `call_flow_zoom_test`, `test_runs_view_test`, `builder_page_test`): pages render, stream, zoom and never overflow.
- **AI engines** (`openai_compat_test`, `cloud_test`, `core_test`): streaming and tool calls for each kind of engine, against a fake server.

Tests ending in `_live_test` need the voice and AI engines running and are run separately.

## Spoken test calls

2,143 scenarios live in [`app/assets/scenarios`](../app/assets/scenarios). They're generated by [`app/test/scenarios/generate.py`](../app/test/scenarios/generate.py).

| Set | Calls | Examples |
|---|---:|---|
| Single calls | 1,150 | "A table for four on Friday at 7pm", "Two margheritas for delivery to 12 Park Road", "Is Mia free Thursday?", "What time do you close?", a full evening, a closed day, out of stock |
| Journeys | 338 | Book, then call back to change it, then someone from another phone tries to cancel it, then cancel; ask for a teammate by name; switch from the restaurant to the barber; book on the website, then by phone |
| Hard calls | 479 | Five-minute calls with detours and small talk; changing their mind twice; rude callers; spelling an unusual name; prompt injection; Spanish, French, German, Italian, Persian, Arabic, Turkish, Polish |
| Security | 176 | See [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) |

How a test call runs:

1. The app sets up the business for real in its own data: the business app with its website, the team of agents with their voices, the skills. Sample bookings that should be there are seeded (for example someone else's table that must not be cancelled).
2. A **simulated caller** (an AI with a persona, a goal and facts) speaks. Its words are spoken with a real voice, then heard back through the app's own speech recognition, so the AI gets what was *heard*, mishearings and all.
3. The AI answers through the same code path as a real call: the same guards, tools and hand-overs. Its answer is spoken and heard back too, to check it comes through clearly.
4. Afterwards, the business app's data is checked:
   - Was exactly one booking saved, with the right date, time, name, phone, table or barber?
   - Was the other person's booking untouched?
   - Was nothing said about anyone else?
   - Did the call end with a goodbye, and was each answer fast enough?

Run them from the app: **Calls → Tests → Run test scenarios**. Choose a quick check, one business, journeys, hard calls, security or everything, and how many calls at once. Watch them live in the same view as real calls. Results go to `test-runs/*.jsonl` in the app's data folder.

No test ever places a real phone call: all test numbers are from ranges reserved for drama.

## Results

From 1,800+ spoken calls run during development:

- **Single calls:** 537 of the 1,150 run. Passing went from **53% to 87%** over six rounds of fixing what failed (214 of 246 in the last full round). The median answer time fell from 10.6 s to under 6 s with one call at a time.
- **Security:** all 176 run. Every scenario has passed at least once after fixes (175 of 176; the last was a usability bug for the genuine customer, since fixed). **No run leaked another person's details or changed their booking.** The best single round passed 44 of 50 (88%); the others failed on speed or on the genuine customer's own cancellation.
- **Journeys:** all 338 run; 99 have passed at least once, and the best round was 22 of 48 (46%). Multi-call, multi-agent journeys are the hardest set. The recent rounds were also run while tracking down a memory regression, and those runs are included.
- **Hard calls:** only a first 10 run so far.
- **First words:** callers hear the first words after about 2.5 s (median, from 7,000+ answers); the full answer takes a median 5–17 s depending on the machine's load. Speed is the main open problem on a 4B model sharing an 18 GB laptop with everything else.
- **Parallel calls:** with three calls at once, the AI, hearing and voice each do 2.2–2.8× the work of a single call in the same time ([`parallel_check.py`](../app/test/scenarios/parallel_check.py)).
- **Models:** see the [model comparison](evaluations/MODELS.md).

Every run is published with all its conversations, tool calls, timings and failures in **[evaluations/RUNS.md](evaluations/RUNS.md)**. These are the raw results of developing the app, including the runs that went badly while a regression was being tracked down.

## Speed

Each answer's time is split into stages (routing, the AI's reply, saving on a yes, the follow-up, the safety net, a hand-over) and logged per turn. That's how slow answers were traced, for example:

- to a model's working memory pushing the computer into swap;
- to prompts that changed every turn and couldn't be reused;
- to too many tools being offered.
